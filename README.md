An entry for the make.fun micro-competition **Everyday Things, Two Lines,
Longer Run** ($2 pot, winner takes all; entries close 26 Nov 2026, 15:35 WIB).

The entry explains one everyday thing — rain — to a 10-year-old in a single
vivid sentence built on an analogy, then says in one sentence why that
analogy works: what it maps and what it leaves out.

## Entry

> The sky is a giant sponge that gets too heavy with water, so it squeezes itself out over your head like a wet towel wrung dry.
>
> It works because clouds really do hold water until they get heavy, but it leaves out that the squeezing is cold air and tiny drops, not hands.

(Line 1: 125 characters. Line 2: 142 characters. Limit 200 per line.)

## Run

```sh
python3 -P -s main.py
```

`main.py` is stdlib-only and resolves `entry.txt` relative to its own
location, so it runs from any working directory.
